# Checkpoints

**WebGL diagnostics in the console.** `src/render/gl-diagnostics.ts` reports under `[atlas:gl]`: the GPU at start (info), every context loss (error) and restore (warning) with the view, sectors and zones resident, quality level, GPU objects, canvas and JS heap, an error when the context is not restored within 5 s, and GL errors such as OUT_OF_MEMORY, read at most once a second. Before, the map handled a loss silently. Verified with `WEBGL_lose_context` on the dev server: loss, restore and the 5 s timeout all appear.

**Labs draw like the game.** The map, the road labs and the lab city share one renderer (`src/render/game-renderer.ts`): look and matcap tune, output pass with FXAA, city lights, adaptive quality, static shadows and the wind clock. The road labs stream the published sectors through `src/lab/lab-city.ts` instead of building their own scene. The Mercado lab compares the game's geometric carriageway with the legacy painted roads on the same sectors. Its old reference mesh (`mercado-carriageway.json`) sat up to ±1 m off the ground and caused the roads that sank into the pavement; it is removed. The sector carriageways are coplanar with the ground within 0.1 mm. [Verification](docs/QA.md#labs-on-the-game-renderer--november-26-2026).

**Car through trees, struck trees.** Tree crowns now mark the stencil like buildings, so the yellow car silhouette also shows when the car is under a crown. Palm fronds do not, because they share the ground material. A car or patrol that hits a tree at more than 1 m/s shakes the crowns and fronds within about 6 m. The shake is faster and wider than the wind, up to 0.7 m with speed, and eases out in about half a second. It is held during hit-stop and cleared on leaving the drive.

**Driving lab panel.** Player values are split into Speed, Steering, Slide and Drift bonus. Camera settings and the aerial and chase views now sit in one Camera folder. The police chase and the patrol cars' body motion have their own folders. View pitch is an angle from 20° to 89° rather than a multiple of the default. Six secondary values left the panel: the kick spring, the gap between hit-stops, the gap between sparks, the pose yaw limit, the shortest bonus drift and the hop per m/s; they are unchanged in `drive-tune.ts`. Flight gains View distance (×0.5–2.5), which stretches the quality stage's horizon and flight zones; the readout shows the horizon in metres.

**Lab consolidation.** Three areas share navigation and collapsible controls. The four road fixtures live under one scenario selector, the driving and flying panel shares the rendering diagnostics, and Copy link preserves the active test settings. The pilot editor is deprecated: no longer linked from the lab navigation, but `editor.html` is still built and reachable by URL. [Entry points](README.md#labs).

Matcap without tilt is the default for the map, drive and flight. In Rendering quality a dropdown chooses Matcap, Base or Sole. Matcap adds a live panel modelled on the Soso Mart one: five matcaps, brightness, street bounce, building and cloud shadow strength, five palettes, edge blur and pink edge glow. Every setting is kept in the URL (`matcap`, `gain`, `bounce`, `shade`, `clouds`, `palette`, `tilt`, `glow`). The desktop Matcap profile starts at static shadows and drops directly to shadows off when needed. `?stats` adds the three.js Stats panel. [Comparison and verification](docs/QA.md#pilot-rendering-benchmark).

The Sole prototype now uses a stronger sky fill for more detail in shade. On the Intel UHD pilot benchmark, Matcap halved median GPU time compared with Base and sustained 60 FPS. Tilt adds three fullscreen draws. [Published and local benchmark](docs/QA.md#pilot-rendering-benchmark).

From the second star, on the straights traveled at high speed, reinforcements can enter off-road along a section of road. The lane is checked from the camera's point of view in the oncoming traffic section. [Test across five neighborhoods](docs/QA.md#city-straight-lines-and-visibility-of-patrols).

Python regeneration reuses projected OSM extracts, roadway masks and meshes, orthophoto tiles, and decoded LiDAR data. Calculation of roof colors only and gzip 6 are available on request. The default generators keep the canonical files. [Guide](docs/EXPAND_CITY.md#reproducible-generation) · [Measurements](docs/QA.md#python-regeneration--november-26-2026).

The daylight of the model is shared between the map, guide, editor and laboratories. The parapets reflect the color of the roofs and the joints of walls, cabins and balconies use the colors of the vertices. [Six fixed-camera comparisons](docs/QA.md#daylight-cover-edges-and-contact).

The clouds pass 400 m above the framed point and fade as they approach the road.

The guide introduces five levels of policing, respite when contact is lost and lateral intercepts on the pilot's road network. From the third level a model can enter that is faster on the straight and wider in turns. The lab records inputs and telemetry to repeat chases. [Gauntlet results](docs/QA.md).

The rendering shares the construction of LOD shapes, pitched roofs and terrain canvases. The streamer calculates the opening state used by the viewer. Reduction of 31 net lines. [Local verification](docs/QA.md).

The viewer has dedicated modules for initial loading and camera calculations. The editor separates history, draft, mesh and gestures. The editor tests also cover canceling dragging when you lose focus or pointer capture. [Local verification](docs/QA.md).

**I · Arcade Steering.** From a standstill, the player reaches 60 km/h in 1.44 s and 90 km/h in another 4 s. The player's roll reaches 28° and the visual bump bump reaches 0.9 m to better distinguish the car's response to the phone's scale.

Climbing and descending change acceleration and braking by up to ±0.6 m/s² for player and patrols. The effect uses the stable terrain elevation and is neutral at bridges and OSM alignments on different levels. [Verification](docs/QA.md#slope-and-acceleration).

**J · Wind and clouds.** A single wind, the trade wind from the north-east, moves together shadows of clouds, crowns and palm fronds (`src/render/wind.ts`). ## Free flight over Las Palmas

The Piper anticipates narrow coverage breaks and maintains absolute altitude while flying over a valley. The camera protects the view of the tail by passing behind the facades and shows the descent path in front of the aircraft during a dive.

The airplane button in the driving HUD starts the Piper takeoff. Arrows, WASD, or one-finger dragging control direction and elevation. The button with the car starts the return to the road, maintaining the coins and destination. Impacts against roofs, ground and sea end the game. [Desktop and mobile tests and views](docs/QA.md#piper).

The Piper's game feel transition makes the propeller speed, the reentry flare and the curve just traveled by the wings visible. Parameters and durations are in [effect catalogue](docs/JUICE.md#piper-attitude-and-trails).

**Quality diagnostics.** The Rendering quality lab shows the reasons for the last four decisions and compares the actual buffer size before and after a DPR change. On the compact profile the last level becomes available only if it produces more pixels than FXAA at scale 1.

**Quality at rest.** With the camera still, the ground profile lowers quality one level after about three seconds below 24 FPS. The ambient loop targets 30 FPS across display refresh rates. [Verification](docs/QA.md#quality-at-rest).

**Drawing buffer transitions.** Adaptive quality applies the new DPR before rendering the frame. The quality lab reports WebGL context loss and restoration. [Regression test](docs/QA.md#controller-decisions-and-webgl-buffers).

**Quality controller review.** Direct reset during a test, calibration management when available cap changes, and separate justification for promotions without sufficient margin. The comparison of the DPR reads the profiles of the city.
